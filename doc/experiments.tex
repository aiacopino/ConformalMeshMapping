Experiments conducted with the two Verfahren of the solver have confirmed the results Wegmann got with regard to convergence: Verfahren 2 typically converges faster, while Verfahren 1 typically yields more accurate results for the inverted ellipse (which is the target region Wegmann investigated; this accuracy statement is not true for other target regions)\cite[§5, last paragraph]{Wegmann1978_newtonverfahren}. This result is expected since Verfahren 2 uses the previous iteration's analytic aproximation to compute the next update. 
Note that we have also looked at more irregular/ difficult examples than Wegmann did, where these results however do not hold:
We present some of our findings in this chapter.
In order to be able to measure the accuracy of the result computed by the solver, we relied on some explicitly known conformal maps, which are provided by Gaier in his book \cite[Anhang 5, p. 264]{Gaier1964}. 
The measure of accuracy in the below tables is calculated as the $L^2$-norm of the difference of the functions at the respective discretisation points.

Next, we consider the strongly non-convex kite region parametrised by     
\begin{equation}
    k(s) = (\text{cos}(s) + 0.65\text{cos}(2s) - 0.65, 1.5 \text{sin}(s)) \text{ for }s \in [0,2\pi].
\end{equation}
The algorithm is only guaranteed to converge locally quadratically for a close enough initial guess. 
However, our experimetns have shown that for the different initial guesses we have tried, the difference is not significant; The solver seems to just take a bit longer for less ideal initial guess.

\bigskip

We further observe that the computed boundary correspondence function, $S_k$, fails to remain injective, directly leading to a loss of conformality in the resulting map. Mathematically, this loss of local univalence occurs when the monotonicity condition is violated, i.e., when $S_k'(\theta) \le 0$. This failure is primarily driven by the crowding phenomenon at the high-curvature tips of the kite domain. To address this instability, several numerical mitigation strategies can be considered:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Adaptive damping: Dynamically adjusting the relaxation parameter during iterations could help maintain injectivity. If an update step causes $S_k$ to become non-injective, the step could be rolled back and a more conservative correction applied instead.
    \item Spectral filtering: The high curvature at the kite's tips may induce Gibbs phenomenon in the Fourier representation of the boundary, leading to oscillations. Implementing a spectral filter like the cutoff parameter for the truncated square but applied inside the newton step of the solver could help suppress high-frequency components that contribute to non-injectivity.
    \item Homotopy continuation: A homotopy approach could be employed, where the convexity parameter $p$ is gradually relaxed from a more convex shape to the target non-convex shape. This would generate a sequence of intermediate mappings, each serving as the initial guess for the next, potentially improving convergence and maintaining injectivity throughout the process.
\end{itemize}
